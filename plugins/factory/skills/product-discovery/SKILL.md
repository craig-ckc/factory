# Product Discovery

Establish that the requested change addresses the real problem. Use proportionate research; do not turn a clear bug fix into a research program.

## Approach

1. Restate who is affected, the problem, and the desired improvement separately from the proposed feature.
2. Examine available user feedback, workflows, analytics, support reports, existing product behavior, or stakeholder context.
3. Distinguish observed facts, reasonable hypotheses, and missing evidence. Never invent interviews, usage data, or user needs.
4. Identify the smallest assumption that could invalidate the proposed solution; test it cheaply when possible.
5. Compare alternatives only when the problem or solution remains materially uncertain.
6. Hand the problem statement, constraints, evidence, and remaining decision to [Requirements](../requirements/SKILL.md).

## Output

A short problem brief: user or actor, current friction, desired outcome, evidence and assumptions, and any recommended change in solution.

## Finish

The next step can explain why the requested outcome matters without assuming the initial feature idea is necessarily correct.
